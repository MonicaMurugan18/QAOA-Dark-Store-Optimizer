The schema and the geography are good. Twelve problems were found in the
numbers, three of which make the optimisation problem meaningless (there is
literally nothing to optimise as the data stands). A thirteenth problem
(an unreachable zone) appears once the others are fixed.

All of these are in the generator script, not in the CSVs. Fix the script and
regenerate; do not hand-edit the CSVs.

CRITICAL PROBLEMS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM 1 - Demand is 10 times too small. Orders and SKU demand contradict
            each other.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   Section 4 of the generator, the zone x SKU demand loop:

            mean_demand = max(1, zone_orders * share
                              * np.random.uniform(0.8, 1.2) / 10)

WHAT IS WRONG
        The "/ 10" is unexplained and wrong. Measured result across all
        20 zones:

            zone_id  avg_daily_orders  total_sku_units  units_per_order
            Z01                   217               33            0.152
            Z02                   288               43            0.149
            Z03                   123               21            0.171
            (min 0.101, mean 0.161, max 0.248 across all zones)

        Every order contains between 0.10 and 0.25 items. That is physically
        impossible - an order contains at least one item. A real quick-commerce
        basket is 4 to 6 items.

        So demand_zones.csv and zone_sku_demand.csv are describing two
        different worlds, and any cost computed from them is meaningless.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        Delete the "/ 10" and multiply by a basket size instead:

            ITEMS_PER_ORDER = 5.0     # define at the top of the file

            mean_units = zone_orders * ITEMS_PER_ORDER * sku_share

RESULT AFTER FIX
        4.42 to 5.24 items per order.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM 2 - The capacity constraint can never be violated. 284x oversupply.
            THIS IS THE MOST SERIOUS ONE.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   Section 1, the max_sku_units line:

            max_sku_units = int(capacity_sqft * np.random.uniform(8, 12))

WHAT IS WRONG
        Measured:
            total daily SKU units demanded :        559
            total max_inventory_units      :    158,673
            ratio                          :     283.9x
            smallest single store          :      8,185 units
                                            (serves the whole city 14x over)

        With capacity this loose, EVERY siting plan is feasible. The optimal
        answer degenerates to "open the one cheapest store and send all
        20 zones to it". There is no allocation decision left, so the
        inventory-allocation half of our project has nothing to solve.

        The deeper issue is a unit mismatch: max_inventory_units is a STOCK
        level, while demand is a DAILY FLOW. They are not comparable without a
        days-of-cover factor.

        For reference, our working Chennai prototype has total demand 1,647
        against total capacity 3,550 (2.2x), and the optimum opens 2 of 4
        stores. That is what a live constraint looks like.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        Build demand FIRST, then size capacity against it. Express both in
        litres so the SKU volumes actually matter:

            CAPACITY_SLACK = 2.2      # total capacity / total daily demand

            total_volume = sum over zones of (units x unit_volume_liters)
            total_sqft   = sum of capacity_sqft over all sites

            daily_capacity_liters[i] = round(
                CAPACITY_SLACK * total_volume * sqft[i] / total_sqft / 25) * 25

        This keeps capacity_sqft meaningful (bigger plot = bigger store) while
        forcing the constraint to bind.

RESULT AFTER FIX
        Capacity / demand = 2.20x. Largest single store holds 0.32x of total
        demand, so at least 4 stores must open. Now there is a real decision.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM 3 - Nine of the ten sites sit exactly on a demand zone, giving
            0.00 minute delivery times.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   The candidate_sites list and the demand_zones list reuse the same
        coordinates for the same neighbourhood names. Also section 5, which
        has no handling time.

WHAT IS WRONG
        These nine pairs all have road_distance_km = 0.0 and
        travel_time_min = 0.0:

            DS01/Z01  DS02/Z03  DS03/Z10  DS04/Z19  DS05/Z18
            DS06/Z13  DS07/Z12  DS08/Z16  DS09/Z11

        Zero-minute delivery is not possible. There is no picking or packing
        time anywhere in the model. These nine pairs will win every assignment
        for free and completely bias the result.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        (a) Add a handling time floor to every pair. Our Chennai model uses
            3 minutes for picking and packing:

                HANDLING_MIN = 3.0
                travel_time_min = HANDLING_MIN + (road_km / speed) * 60

        (b) Offset the site coordinates so a candidate PLOT is near a
            neighbourhood but not identical to the zone centroid. About 390 m
            is enough:

                SITE_OFFSET_DEG = 0.0035
                angle = 2 * pi * i / number_of_sites
                site_lat = lat + SITE_OFFSET_DEG * cos(angle)
                site_lon = lon + SITE_OFFSET_DEG * sin(angle)

            Rename them "White Town (plot)" etc. so it is clear these are
            candidate properties, not the neighbourhood itself.

RESULT AFTER FIX
        Zero 0-minute pairs. Fastest delivery is 4.19 minutes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM 13 - One demand zone has no store that can reach it in time.
             (This only becomes visible after problems 1-3 are fixed.)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS WRONG
        With correct travel times, Z20 (Auroville Fringe) has NO candidate site
        inside the 15-minute promise:

            nearest plot DS04 -> 15.13 minutes, against a 15.00 minute promise

        It misses by eight seconds. An unreachable zone makes the instance
        ill posed: no solution can serve everybody, so the model is broken
        before it starts.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        Add a real candidate site that covers the north. Kuilapalayam is the
        village on Auroville's edge:

            ("DS11", "Kuilapalayam", 11.9958, 79.8175)

        DO NOT fix this by relaxing the SLA to 16 minutes. The 15-minute
        promise is the defining constraint of the whole project - the data
        should fit the promise, not the other way round.

        Also add a permanent check at the end of the generator so this can
        never slip through again:

            zones_with_a_server = travel[travel.within_sla].zone_id.nunique()
            assert zones_with_a_server == len(demand_zones), "unreachable zone"

RESULT AFTER FIX
        20 of 20 zones covered. Servers per zone: min 1, median 4, max 7.

PROBLEM 4 - Category shares add up to 1.18 instead of 1.00.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   Section 4, the category_share dictionary.

WHAT IS WRONG
        The dictionary values sum to 1.00 across the seven categories. But
        Dairy contains TWO SKUs (SKU01 Fresh Milk and SKU03 Eggs), and the
        full 0.18 Dairy share is applied to each of them separately.

            sum over categories : 1.00
            sum applied per-SKU : 1.18

        Dairy gets double weight and all demand totals are 18% too high.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        Divide each category's share among the SKUs in that category:

            n_in_category = count of SKUs per category
            sku_share[sku] = CATEGORY_SHARE[category] / n_in_category[category]

        So Dairy becomes 0.09 for milk and 0.09 for eggs.

        Add an assertion so it cannot drift again:

            assert abs(sum(sku_share.values()) - 1.0) < 1e-9

RESULT AFTER FIX
        Shares sum to exactly 1.0000.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM 5 - unit_volume_liters exists in skus.csv but is never used.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS WRONG
        SKU volumes range from 0.15 litres (potato chips) to 1.20 litres
        (bananas), but store capacity is a flat unit COUNT. So a packet of
        chips takes the same shelf space as a bottle of water. The column is
        present and dead.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        Carry volume through to demand and express capacity in litres:

            demand_volume_liters = avg_daily_demand_units * unit_volume_liters

        Add demand_volume_liters as a column in zone_sku_demand.csv, and use
        daily_capacity_liters in candidate_dark_stores.csv (see problem 2).

RESULT AFTER FIX
        14,422 litres per day total demand, directly comparable to capacity.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM 6 - Rent has almost nothing to do with store size or location.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   Section 1:

            rent = np.random.randint(35000, 90000)
            setup_cost = np.random.randint(250000, 650000)
            capacity_sqft = np.random.choice([1000, 1200, 1500, 1800, 2000])

WHAT IS WRONG
        These are three completely independent random draws.

            correlation(rent, capacity_sqft)  = 0.262
            correlation(setup, capacity_sqft) = 0.285

        Concretely:
            DS07 Reddiarpalayam  1,200 sqft  Rs 76,434/month  = Rs 63.7/sqft
            DS04 Kalapet         2,000 sqft  Rs 51,023/month  = Rs 25.5/sqft

        A 2.5x price-per-sqft inversion, and White Town (the premium French
        Quarter) comes out cheaper per sqft than Reddiarpalayam.

        This means the choice of which store to open is decided by the random
        number generator, not by geography or economics. The whole point of
        the project is to make that decision meaningfully.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        Make rent a price per square foot times a location premium based on
        distance to the town centre:

            RENT_PER_SQFT_BASE = 35.0          # INR / sqft / month
            TOWN_CENTRE = (11.9330, 79.8340)   # White Town

            d = haversine_km(site_lat, site_lon, *TOWN_CENTRE)
            premium = clip(1.35 - 0.05 * d, 0.80, 1.35)
            rent = sqft * RENT_PER_SQFT_BASE * premium * uniform(0.95, 1.05)

        And make setup cost proportional to floor area:

            setup = sqft * uniform(280, 360)    # INR / sqft fit-out

RESULT AFTER FIX
        correlation(rent, sqft) = 0.907. Rent per sqft ranges Rs 29.5 to 48.0
        (was Rs 25.5 to 63.7), with the premium now tracking distance from the
        centre as it should.

PROBLEM 8 - Travel speed is randomised separately for every site-zone pair.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   Section 5, inside the double loop:

            speed = AVG_SPEED_KMPH[ztype] * np.random.uniform(0.9, 1.1)

WHAT IS WRONG
        A fresh speed is drawn for each of the 200 pairs. Measured implied
        speeds within a single zone type:

            commercial   16.54 to 19.73 km/h
            mixed        18.97 to 22.90 km/h
            residential  21.65 to 26.37 km/h

        Consequences: travel time is NOT a function of distance (two pairs at
        the same distance get different times), and the time matrix violates
        the triangle inequality. That is not a road network.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        Draw one speed per ZONE, before the loop, and reuse it:

            zone_speed[zone_id] = AVG_SPEED_KMPH[ztype] * uniform(0.92, 1.08)

        Store it as a column (avg_road_speed_kmph) in demand_zones.csv so it
        is auditable.

RESULT AFTER FIX
        Speed spread within a zone: 0.02 km/h (was up to 3.2 km/h).

PROBLEM 9 - There is no SLA / delivery promise anywhere in the data.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS WRONG
        The 10-to-15-minute delivery promise is the defining constraint of
        hyperlocal quick commerce, and the single thing that makes our quantum
        encoding small enough to run. It is not in the dataset at all.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        Define it and mark every pair:

            SERVICE_PROMISE_MIN = 15.0
            within_sla = (delivery_time_min <= SERVICE_PROMISE_MIN)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM 10 - Rent is monthly, setup is one-time, and they cannot be added.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS WRONG
        The objective function needs ONE number per store for "cost of opening
        it". We have a monthly rent and a one-time fit-out cost in different
        time units, with no amortisation horizon given.

CHANGE TO MAKE
        Add a derived column with the horizon stated explicitly:

            AMORTISE_MONTHS = 36        # 3-year horizon
            DAYS_PER_MONTH  = 30.4

            daily_opening_cost_inr = rent / DAYS_PER_MONTH
                                   + setup / (AMORTISE_MONTHS * DAYS_PER_MONTH)

RESULT AFTER FIX
        Rs 1,416 to Rs 3,394 per day across the 11 sites.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM 11 - demand_std_dev contradicts the process that generated the demand.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   Section 4:

            demand_std = round(avg_daily_demand * np.random.uniform(0.15, 0.35), 2)

WHAT IS WRONG
        Demand is generated from a Poisson distribution, whose standard
        deviation is sqrt(mean). But the std column is a separate, unrelated
        15-35% of the realised value.

        For a mean of 4: true Poisson std is 2.0, the file says about 1.0.

        If anyone later does stochastic or robust optimisation with these
        numbers, the mean and the std are describing different distributions.

CHANGE TO MAKE
            demand_std_dev = round(sqrt(mean_units), 2)

        If we want overdispersion (real demand is burstier than Poisson),
        switch to a negative binomial and state the dispersion parameter.
        Do not just invent a std.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM 12 - The "+1" inflates every demand value.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   Section 4:

            avg_daily_demand = int(np.random.poisson(mean_demand) + 1)

WHAT IS WRONG
        Adding 1 shifts the whole distribution, so the realised mean is
        mean + 1, not mean. With means around 3 to 5, that is a 20-30%
        inflation on top of everything else.

CHANGE TO MAKE
            units = max(1, int(rng.poisson(mean_units)))

        max(1, ...) keeps demand positive without shifting the distribution.

================================================================================
ONE MORE THING: SIZE
================================================================================
Even after every fix, the full instance is too large to run QAOA on:

    11 sites x 20 zones -> 76 SLA-feasible pairs -> 87 qubits

Our laptop simulation wall is about 21 qubits (2^21 states). 87 qubits is
astronomically beyond that.

So we need TWO datasets from the same generator:

    Full instance (11 x 20)  -> classical baselines only
                                (greedy, simulated annealing, exact)

    Sub-instance (4 x 5)     -> 15 SLA-feasible pairs -> 19 qubits
                                small enough to simulate QAOA

IMPORTANT: when carving out the sub-instance, re-size its capacities against
ITS OWN demand. Otherwise problem 2 comes straight back at the sub-instance
level and the capacity constraint stops binding again.

Current sub-instance: sites DS01, DS02, DS07, DS08 and zones Z01, Z03, Z05,
Z07, Z16. Demand 2,851 L/day, capacity 6,250 L/day (2.19x), at least 2 stores
must open, all 5 zones covered.
